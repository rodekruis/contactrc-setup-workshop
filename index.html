Workshop ContactRC setup

ContactRC is the IFRC contact center, built with support from the Netherlands Red Cross. It runs on Amazon Connect. People call one number and your team answers from a browser.

Today we decide what your contact center setup is and write what callers hear. You know your callers; we help you configure the system. No technical knowledge is needed.

Your setup

Three choices that shape your contact center

We make these first. They decide which flows you need and how many messages you write. Write down what you decide in the note under each choice.

DECISION 1

How many languages?

One language keeps it simple: no language menu and one set of messages. Every extra language adds complexity: a language menu, every message written, translated and checked in each language, and agents who can answer in that language.

Netherlands: Dutch is the default language; English, Arabic and Turkish are in the menu.

DECISION 2

How does your team log in?

We propose user accounts managed in AWS (IAM). If you have a strong reason to use your own login system instead, tell us today.

Proposal: AWS IAM accounts, unless you strongly prefer otherwise.

DECISION 3

Do you record calls?

Recording helps with quality and training, but it has privacy consequences. Decide this with your data protection officer.

Attachments are kept for 24 hours, then deleted.

Netherlands: calls are recorded.

CHECK

Do you agree with the computer voice?

All messages are spoken by the computer voice, Amazon Polly. Together we listen to it in your languages and you confirm it is good enough.

Open Amazon Polly (log in to AWS first)

How it works

Every call passes the same checks, in this order

At each step the system uses something you give us: a message, an opening time, a list. Choose one or several languages to see what changes. Each step links to its call flow further down.

    One call at a time. A team member handles one call at a time. The caller's earlier contacts show on screen, so nobody has to tell their story twice.
    For your information

    Four switches your supervisors control

    Supervisors can turn these on or off themselves, at any moment. There is nothing to decide today.

    Emergency closure

    Close the line immediately, for example during an evacuation or an outage. Callers hear the emergency message.

    Temporary message

    Add a short announcement before the queue. Useful when many people call about the same thing.

    Callback

    Offer or stop offering callbacks on a long wait. When a callback is offered is fixed.

    Survey

    Turn the satisfaction survey after the call on or off.

    Call flows · Sheet A

    Write what callers hear, scenario by scenario

    Each scenario shows one path through the call. Write your text in the purple boxes, in one working language. Translation comes after the workshop. A message that appears in several scenarios is shared: write it once and it fills in everywhere. [X] marks a value the system says for you, such as the wait time.

    While writing: write for the ear, not the eye

    You already know how to talk to people in difficult situations. These tips are specific to phone messages.

    • Read it out loudIf it sounds stiff when you say it, it sounds worse from a computer voice. We play each message in the computer voice to check.
    • Most important firstCallers stop listening after the first sentence. Lead with what they need to know or do.
    • Option before numberPeople wait for their option, then listen for the key."For Arabic, press 3."
    • Short and one ideaAim for under 15 seconds per message. No more than four menu options.
    • No promises you cannot keepAvoid "shortly" or "today" unless that is true every time.
    • Always a next stepWhen you are closed, say when you open and where else people can find help.
